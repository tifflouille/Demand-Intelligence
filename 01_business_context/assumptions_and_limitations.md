# Assumptions & Limitations

## Initial Assumptions

* Completed order items are treated as observed purchasing demand.
* Items sold are used as the primary measure of demand, with orders and revenue as supporting indicators.
* Monthly aggregation is appropriate for analysing demand trends and volatility.
* Cancelled or otherwise uncompleted orders will be excluded from demand calculations.
* Product categories can be used as a meaningful level of commercial analysis.
* Historical demand patterns provide some useful information for future planning.

These assumptions may be revised following data validation and exploratory analysis.

## Known Limitations

* **No reliable inventory data:** Stock levels and stockouts are not available, so unmet demand cannot be measured.
* **No inventory economics:** Inventory costs, lead times and supply constraints are unavailable, preventing detailed inventory optimisation.
* **No marketing data:** Marketing spend, campaigns and promotional activity are not available, so their impact on demand cannot be isolated.
* **Marketplace dataset:** Olist represents a marketplace rather than a single DTC business, so seller and product dynamics may differ from a typical ecommerce brand.
* **Limited historical period:** The available data limits the ability to identify long-term trends and recurring seasonality.
* **Observed sales are not true demand:** Purchases reflect realised sales and may not capture customers who wanted to purchase but could not.
* **Product-level data availability:** Some products may have insufficient sales history for reliable forecasting.
